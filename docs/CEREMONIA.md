# The circuit ceremony

> **Status as of 2026-09-17: the current ceremony is a development ceremony and
> is not fit for production.** It was generated on a single machine, by a single
> person. Before using OpenPay with real data it must be redone as described
> below.

## What it is and why it exists

OpenPay's circuit uses **Groth16**, which needs two keys to be generated before
it can be used: one to build proofs and one to verify them. That generation
starts from a random number that **must be destroyed**.

If someone keeps that number, they can **forge proofs of false statements**: for
example, prove that every payment went to authorized vendors when it did not.

Two clarifications that matter, and that should not be overstated in either
direction:

- What breaks is **soundness**, not privacy. With the number one can forge
  proofs, but **cannot uncover anyone's data**. Amounts, vendors and dates
  remain unrecoverable.
- This is why the keys are generated by **several participants**: it is enough
  for **a single one** to have destroyed their part for the whole to be secure.
  You do not have to trust everyone; you have to trust that at least one was
  honest.

## The two phases

| Phase | What it covers | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Powers of Tau** | Universal: works for any circuit up to a certain size | Normally a large public ceremony is reused |
| **2 — Circuit setup** | Specific to *this* circuit; changes if the circuit changes | The project runs it |

## Why phase 1 is generated locally today

The normal practice is **not** to generate phase 1 but to download Hermez's,
which had thousands of participants. On **September 17, 2026**, the three known
mirrors answered `AccessDenied`:

- `https://storage.googleapis.com/zkevm/ptau/powersOfTau28_hez_final_18.ptau`
- `https://hermez.s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/powersOfTau28_hez_final_18.ptau`
- `https://pse-trusted-setup-ppot.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/hermez-raw-18`

So `scripts/build.sh` generates phase 1 on the machine where it runs. That is
good for development and for the repository's tests to pass, **and for nothing
else**.

## What has to be done before using this for real

- [ ] **Obtain a public 2^18 Powers of Tau.** Find a live mirror of the Hermez
      file or of the Perpetual Powers of Tau, and check its hash against the one
      published by the original ceremony. This is the first thing to solve:
      without it, everything else rests on a home-made phase 1.
- [ ] **Run phase 2 as a ceremony with several participants.** At minimum the
      project author and three or four people from outside the project — a
      professor, someone from a citizen oversight group, someone from a
      foundation. Each one runs `snarkjs zkey contribute` on the previous
      participant's output.
- [ ] **Publish every contribution** with its hash and the name of whoever made
      it, so that anyone can rebuild the chain and check that their contribution
      is in it.
- [ ] **Anchor the fingerprint of the verification key** in `CommitmentRegistry`
      (`RootKind.VerificationKey`), so that the public portal does not have to
      be taken at its word.
- [ ] **Redo the ceremony if the circuit changes.** Any modification to
      `authorized_within_budget.circom` invalidates the previous phase 2.

## How a contribution is run

```sh
# Each participant starts from the previous one's file and hands over their own
npx snarkjs zkey contribute previous.zkey my_contribution.zkey \
  --name="Name of the contributor"
# It asks for entropy from the keyboard: type randomly for a good while

# Anyone can check the whole chain
npx snarkjs zkey verify circuit.r1cs pot18_final.ptau final.zkey
```

## How to explain this

Not as a hidden technical detail, but as part of the argument. The honest
question a reviewer can ask is *"and why should I believe your proof?"*, and the
answer has three parts:

1. The circuit is public and can be read: it says exactly what is being proven.
2. The ceremony was distributed and its contributions are published: it is
   enough for one of the participants to have been honest.
3. The key fingerprint is anchored on-chain: the portal cannot serve a different
   key without it being noticed.

As long as the second and the third are not done, say so. A system that presents
itself as verifiable and hides its weak point is worse than one that declares
it.
